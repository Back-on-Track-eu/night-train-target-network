# About the Target Network

Europe was once crossed by night trains. Back-on-Track works to bring them
back at scale, and this tool is where the network gets designed in the open.

## Why night trains

For journeys between 500 and 3,000 kilometres the night train is the
realistic alternative to flying, and flying is the most climate-damaging way
to travel. In the EU, aircraft emit roughly 28 times more greenhouse gas per
passenger than long-distance trains ([IEA figures, via our position
paper][paper]). Aviation will not decarbonise on the timescale that matters.
Sleeping your way across the continent already works.

## Target Network 2032

Europe runs about 150 night train routes today. Our [general position
paper][paper] puts the number Europe needs at 300, enough to triple passenger
numbers within seven years and connect every major city.

Which 300 is the open question, and it deserves an answer with evidence
attached. Target Network 2032 is our proposal: a network built route by
route, each one carrying its own economics and climate effect, addressed to
the people who decide European and national transport policy. Almost all of
it runs on track that already exists. What is missing is the network design,
the right train concepts, fair operating conditions, and the funding to close
the gap.

## What the tool does

Draw a route on the map, set the stops and departure times, pick a train, and
the model returns what the service would cost to run for a year, what it
would earn under a demand assumption you can change, and the emissions it
would displace. It takes about five minutes and no expertise.

Three kinds of contribution are useful here:

- **Travellers** know which connections Europe is missing. Propose the night
  train you would take.
- **Practitioners** know what these services actually cost. Operations,
  rolling stock, infrastructure and demand are all modelled from parameters
  you can challenge, with the sources named on every page.
- **Policy stakeholders** need route-level figures rather than a wish list:
  cost, subsidy requirement, passengers shifted off aircraft, CO₂ avoided.

The knowledge this network needs is not in one office, which is why the
design is crowdsourced and the model is open source.

## What happens to a proposal

Submissions are assessed on the measures that decide transport policy:
climate impact, demand, and cost to the public. The strongest become the
backbone of the target network. We close the remaining gaps, refine the train
concepts, and publish the result as a study with a public map and a stated
method. Contributors are credited.

## What this site documents

The rest of these pages are the model behind those figures: every formula,
the parameters it reads, the source each parameter came from, and the
assumptions still standing in for data.

Before quoting anything from the tool, read [known gaps](/not-modelled),
which lists what the model does not yet cover and what each omission
affects.
