# How shunting and stabling were priced

This domain covers [shunting](/cost/shunting) — moving the train around
stations and yards — and [overnight parking](/cost/parking), the stabling
of a train between two nights of service.

## Provenance, stated plainly

**Only 14 values are sourced.** Seventeen of twenty-eight countries fall
back on a European default rather than a national figure. This is the
weakest-sourced of the charge domains apart from station charges, and it
is worth knowing before quoting either line.

## The assumption that dominates it

Infrastructure managers publish what _they_ charge for a shunting
movement, but that is rarely the whole cost: it does not include the
staff, the locomotive time, or the yard capacity that a real operator has
to procure elsewhere. The calibration adds a **market top-up** on top of
the published charge — **190 EUR** per movement in higher-cost countries,
**110 EUR** in lower-cost ones — carrying a stated band of **±35%**.

That top-up is larger than many of the published charges it sits on top
of. So the shunting line is, in substance, an assumption with a published
charge attached rather than the other way round. The ±35% band is the
honest width of it.

Compounding this: the [number of shunting movements](/cost/shunting) is
itself fixed at two per trip rather than derived from the shape of the
service.

## Stabling

Priced on each country's own basis against the scheduled layover and the
train's length. Several countries provide a free allowance that covers an
ordinary twelve-hour turnaround entirely — Norway and Croatia among them —
so their parking cost is genuinely zero rather than missing.

Power drawn while standing is charged on top, at a rate per stabled hour.
A night train stabled all day with its systems live is not free to park
even where the track occupation is.

## What this replaced

Until this calibration, shunting was a flat 575 EUR per event everywhere.
The calibrated range is roughly 115–321 EUR across Europe, so most routes
became cheaper here — and the flat parking rate that preceded it is now
display-only, kept for reference but not read by the cost model.
