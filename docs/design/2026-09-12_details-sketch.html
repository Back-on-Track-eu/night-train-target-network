Details — sketch, round 6

Five tabs: Supply · Train operation · Infrastructure · Overhead · Demand. Supply is two panels now — Schedule, and Places and prices in one table. One ⓘ per panel. Every cost panel is a Kassenzettel: per-trip lines with their share, then the trip → cycle → year strip.

Sketch, not code. Route Berlin – Verona, 1,080 km, 9 stops, DE · AT · IT. marks a number the backend does not return today — I invented a plausible value so the layout is sized against realistic content. Everything else comes from the handover's operations example or the real NEW-BAL-7 catalog entry. Laid out for the width at which Details is available at all — the lg breakpoint and up, 1,024 px; below that the mobile card stays as today. Every panel clips its own content (overflow: hidden, tables scroll inside) so nothing can run out of a box. The page loads with the handover's example edits so the stale state is visible — press Recalculate in the top row to commit them. Hover any ⓘ.

How a change propagates. Two inputs change the calculation: the schedule and the prices, both on Supply. Change one and the panel that owns it previews live — its own figures recompute on the page and are marked as a preview. Every other panel whose figures depend on that input greys out and carries an awaiting recalculation badge, holding its figures rather than showing half-updated ones; the tab it sits on gets a dot. Panels that do not depend on the change stay untouched — change a price and Infrastructure is unaffected. Minimum turnaround is a model constant (180 min), displayed under Train operation but not editable, so it never puts anything out of date.

What every ⓘ opens — InfoHint as today, plus the docPath line:Physical rakes for the busiest month: the cycle days times that month's days-per-week, rounded up. Costed as this figure divided by coach availability.Read more in the documentation

What this rests on

Passenger numbers are an interim assumption, not a demand model: every train is filled to the same share of its places on every leg. Competing modes stay fixed factors until the demand model lands.

When it does, this tab shows demand by OD pair, by class and by mode shifted from — and the prices under Supply get elasticities.
