Details — Supply and Demand, sketch round 7

The demand model will not land for V1. Demand becomes the third input of the Details card, next to the schedule and the prices: how many places each traveller group asks for, and how those sales spread over the route's OD pairs. Supply is drawn with the schedule reduced to one average frequency — the month grid is gone.

Sketch, not code. Route Berlin – Verona, 1,080 km, 9 stops, DE · AT · IT; 17 sellable OD pairs. The schedule is one average frequency in days per week; nothing seasonal remains, so a month is a twelfth of the year. Compositions and their places by class are the real catalog (COMPOSITION_CATALOG_OVERVIEW); base-fare defaults are the ones decided 2026-09-18 (Seat 10 € + 0.06 €/km, Couchette 75 € + 0.03, Sleeper 125 € + 0.04, Capsule 75 € + 0.03); services and catering stay at DEMAND 0.0.5. Everything on the Demand tab is client-side arithmetic on the page, so it previews live and is marked as a preview; only Recalculate commits. The page opens on the defaults — Medium, 200,000 passengers a year, the default group mix, 3 days per week, an even spread over OD pairs — so the first thing to try is a demand level, a group figure, or a distribution slider. Hover any ⓘ.

What changes in the change-scope rule. A third scope, demand, joins schedule and prices. Demand owns its panels and previews them; Train operation (subsidy), Overhead (EBIT on revenue) and the What-follows panel on Supply depend on it — that panel previews the schedule and prices live against the committed demand. The request would carry schedule.days_per_week (one figure), demand.passengers_per_year by traveller group and demand.od {stop_weights, pinned_shares}; the class preferences, the group order, the 50 / 20 / 20 / 10 round shares and the 80 % rule share are model constants in place of the model constant STOPGAP_UTILIZATION_PER; demand is stored as an absolute yearly figure, so a family evaluates every composition — and every frequency — against the same demand — which is exactly what the ladders show.

Decisions (all confirmed 2026-09-18)

  1. Demand levels are defaults, not a scenario switch — and the chosen level is saved with the proposal. A level writes the total and keeps the group mix; any edit afterwards makes it Custom, and Custom is what gets saved then. The four totals are model constants (DEMAND 0.1.0).
  2. Unit of the input. Places sold per departure (drawn here) keeps the ladder honest — utilisation is a per-departure idea. Per year would tie the input to the schedule and change under a schedule edit.
  3. Stored as places, not as a share. A family evaluates all compositions against one request, so an absolute demand is the only thing that makes the ladders true. The share is just how it is entered.
  4. Rounds, not spill. Each group only ever sits in the classes it lists. The 50 / 20 / 20 / 10 are the shares of a group's demand released per round, so the groups interleave: Leisure – comfort places half of its demand, then Leisure – group half of its, and so on, before anyone places the next fifth. Within a round 80 % of a group fills its first preference before touching its second; the other 20 % go evenly to the group's other listed classes (a variance term as expected values, not random draws, so results stay reproducible; a single-class group has no other class). Demand no listed class can seat is not served — on REF-POD-14 (no Seat, no Couchette) that is Leisure – group and Leisure – budget in full.
  5. Default group mix is Leisure – comfort 50 %, Leisure – group 25 %, Senior leisure 10 %, Leisure – budget 10 %, Business 5 %; a level applies it to the total, and editing a group changes the mix for that proposal only.
  6. One distribution for all classes. Sleeper demand plausibly skews long. A per-class curve is a small extension of od_distribution; the sketch keeps one to stay readable.
  7. The year is fixed, the trip follows. Demand is stored per year; per trip is that figure over the departures the frequency yields, per month a twelfth of the year — no seasons. So a frequency change moves the load per departure, not the input: every second day fills each train roughly twice as full and turns demand it can no longer seat into "not served". The frequency stays a Supply › Schedule input; the utilisation panel names it with a link, since it decides how many trips carry the year.
  8. Backend contract. distribute_demand() already takes a utilisation and a fare map; it grows a per-class places figure and a weight function over OD distance. CALC_VERSION patch bump, FAMILY_DOCUMENT_FORMAT +1, the family key gains the demand fields.
